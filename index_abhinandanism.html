Abhinandanism:
a political framework.

Abhinandanism or Dominionism is a Third Positionist political ideology that synthesizes elements of patriotic nationalism, militarism, and civilizational revivalism rooted in Sanatan Dharma. It advocates for a centralized, authoritarian form of governance led by a morally upright and strong military leader, with the core aim of restoring India’s historical and spiritual greatness. The ideology positions itself as a post-democratic, post-colonial response to perceived internal divisions and external threats to national sovereignty and cultural integrity.

It combines cultural traditionalism with statist authoritarianism, rejecting both liberal democratic pluralism and Marxist collectivism.

01 / Key features

Key Features of Abhinandanism (in Political Science Terms):

Each of the twelve features is kept as a distinct numbered entry.

01

Authoritarian Militarism

Advocates for a military-led state apparatus, wherein the armed forces act as the guarantors of national unity, integrity, and discipline. Civilian leadership is subordinated to a singular commander-statesman role combining executive and symbolic authority.

02

Cultural Traditionalism and Civilizational Nationalism

Emphasizes the primacy of Sanatan Dharma as a cultural and ethical foundation for national life. Seeks to revive Indigenous traditions, rejecting imported ideologies, religions, and colonial social constructs.

03

Mandatory Conscription

Promotes compulsory military service for citizens, especially the Hindu majority, as both a rite of passage and a tool for national integration and discipline.

04

Religious Exclusionism

Defines Indian identity in ethnocultural-religious terms, asserting that non-indigenous religions (Islam, Christianity) are incompatible with the national ethos. Supports a Hindu-first civilizational framework, wherein religious minorities are either assimilated or politically marginalized.

05

Anti-Caste Positionality

Rejects the modern caste-based reservation system, viewing it as a colonial and Ambedkarite construct designed to divide Hindus. Reaffirms the Varna system as a natural, dharmic social order, though reinterpreted for meritocratic use.

06

Expansionist Geopolitics (Bharatiya Expansionism)

Supports the idea of "Greater Bharat", asserting historical and civilizational claims over regions culturally linked to the Indian subcontinent. Aims to project India as a regional hegemon and eventual civilizational superpower through hard and soft power tools.

07

Anti-Communism

Rejects Marxist and leftist ideologies, viewing them as materialist, anti-national, and atheistic, threatening to traditional social order.

08

Anti-Capitalism

Opposes unrestrained capitalism, critiquing its inequity, corporate exploitation, and moral erosion. Advocates for a controlled, national-interest-driven economy, possibly blending state corporatism with indigenous economic models.

09

Anti-Secularism

Views secularism as a Western import that dilutes national identity. Calls for a "Dharmic State" where laws and institutions reflect civilizational values rather than neutrality in religious matters.

10

Vedic+Finnish Education System

Students would progress when they master knowledge, not when they beat classmates. Vedic+Finnish Education System provides the best ground for growth, education and also, emotional well-being.

11

A Semi-Closed Economy

A civilization producing scientists, philosophers, engineers, and leaders, rather than just employees for global markets. Total Overhaul of the Indian Education System and Examination System.

12

Promotion of Indian Architecture:

Why follow Persian, Arabic, Turkish, Chinese Architectures when we have one of our own, Dravidian, North-Indian, etc. Anything outside of the Indosphere will not be tolerated as such.

02 / Classification

Ideological Classification (Political Spectrum):

Authoritarian Right

Strong state power, hierarchical order, cultural supremacy.

Third Positionist

Rejects both left-wing internationalism and right-wing liberal capitalism.

Civilizational Statism

Views the state as an embodiment of national spirit rather than a contract-based liberal institution.

Summary of Similarities:

  • Both are anti-liberal, nationalistic, militaristic, and support centralized leadership.
  • Both use history, culture, and religion as pillars of state identity.
  • Both seek to restore lost glory and resist foreign ideological or institutional influence.

Key Difference:

  • Abhinandanism is more ideological and civilizational, based on a spiritual-cultural worldview.
  • Putinism is more pragmatic and geopolitical, focusing on Russian power, security, and global positioning.
