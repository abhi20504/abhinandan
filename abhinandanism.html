Abhinandanism:
the stated framework.

This page presents Abhinandanism, also called Dominionism in the supplied reference document, as a political ideology combining civilizational nationalism, Sanatan Dharma, centralized authority, militarism and a state-guided economy.

01 / Overview

What is Abhinandanism?

The reference describes Abhinandanism as a Third Positionist ideology intended to restore India’s historical and spiritual standing. It places national sovereignty, cultural continuity, discipline and a centralized state at the centre of its political vision.

01

Centralized leadership

The document proposes a singular commander-statesman, described as a “Deshpati,” combining executive and symbolic authority, with the military serving as a guarantor of national unity.

02

Civilizational nationalism

Sanatan Dharma and indigenous traditions are presented as the ethical and cultural foundation of national life.

03

Third Position economics

The text rejects both unrestrained capitalism and Marxist collectivism, instead describing a controlled, national-interest-driven and state-guided economy.

04

National service

Compulsory military service is proposed as a means of discipline, shared duty and national integration.

02 / Key features

The policy architecture.

The PDF lists the following features. Some proposals, particularly those concerning religious minorities, caste, democratic governance and territorial expansion, are exclusionary or coercive in their stated form and are presented here as positions of the source document.

01 · Authoritarian militarism

Military-led institutions and a centralized commander-statesman model.

02 · Cultural traditionalism

Sanatan Dharma as a national ethical-cultural foundation; revival of indigenous traditions.

03 · Mandatory conscription

Compulsory service proposed as a rite of passage and instrument of integration.

04 · Religious exclusionism

The source defines national identity in Hindu-first terms and proposes assimilation or political marginalization of non-indigenous religious communities.

05 · Varna and reservations

The PDF rejects caste-based reservations and advocates a reinterpreted Varna order. It does not set out a detailed implementation framework.

06 · Bharatiya expansionism

The document advances a “Greater Bharat” vision based on claimed historical and civilizational connections.

07 · Anti-communism and anti-capitalism

It criticizes Marxist materialism and unrestrained corporate capitalism, proposing a state-guided economy.

08 · Anti-secularism

It rejects religious neutrality in the state and proposes a “Dharmic State.”

09 · Vedic–Finnish education

Students would progress by mastering knowledge rather than competing against classmates, with emotional well-being named as a goal.

10 · Semi-closed economy

The text calls for education and economic restructuring to develop scientists, philosophers, engineers and leaders.

11 · Indian architectural traditions

It prioritizes Indian architectural traditions, including Dravidian and North Indian styles, and opposes architectural influence from outside the Indosphere.

03 / Classification

Where the document places it.

The PDF uses three labels to describe the ideology’s position on the political spectrum.

Authoritarian right

Emphasis on strong state power, hierarchy and cultural primacy.

Third Positionist

Presented as a rejection of both left-wing internationalism and right-wing liberal capitalism.

Civilizational statism

The state is described as an expression of national spirit rather than a contract-based liberal institution.

05 / Context

An evolving political document.

This page is a web presentation of the supplied reference, not an official party manifesto or a claim that every proposal has been implemented. The source outlines an authoritarian and religiously exclusionary political programme; readers should assess its implications for democratic accountability, equal citizenship, religious freedom and individual rights.
